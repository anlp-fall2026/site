## Guidelines for Picking a Topic

Your project should be a novel (if small) contribution in NLP. Successful projects generally fall into one of these two broad categories:

1. New Method, Existing Problem
    - Propose a new technique—such as architecture modifications, different training/inference paradigms or objective functions, data augmentation, inference strategies—for solving a well-established NLP task. The emphasis here is on technical innovation and sophistication, ideally building on advanced concepts introduced in the course.
2. Applying/Adapting Existing Methods to a New Problem
    - Apply known NLP methods to a new task, domain, or language. The novelty in this type of project lies in problem formulation and thoughtful adaptation—tailoring existing methods to address the unique challenges of the new context. To help brainstorm, it can be useful to consider how ideas popular in certain areas of NLP/ML may be applicable to a new task or domain. Alternatively, depending on your topics of interest, you might consider how theoretical frameworks or concepts from other fields–such as cognitive science, social sciences, or the humanities–could be usefully operationalized in an NLP context. Finally, we encourage you to talk to your classmates and the TAs to help generate and refine ideas!

We recommend starting by conducting a literature review on a topic of interest. Then, based on the gaps, trends, or opportunities identified, propose a relevant and feasible project topic.
We encourage you to come to office hours to discuss directions with us.


## Deliverables

### Abstract (due November 3, 11:59 PM)
Submit a title, abstract, and list of team members (if working in a team).
Your abstract should be one paragraph (maximum 200 words) describing your proposed project.
The title and abstract will be used to pair you with a TA who has relevant interests.

You may not change your team composition or project title after the abstract deadline.

### Project Propsal (due November 10, 11:59 PM)

Submit a project proposal that contains each of the following key elements.

1. Abstract
    - It's fine to copy and paste this from the Abstract deadline
2. Motivation
    - What is the general research topic?
    - What is the significance of the research topic?
    - Why is this topic worth working on?
3. Literature Review
    - Provide a broad view of the relevant research landscape Provide concrete descriptions on a small handful of the most highly related works.
    - Identify major trends/patterns across prior work.
    - Identify shortcomings with prior work and/or other opportunities that motivate this work
    - While we encourage you to use AI to help you find relevent papers, this section should be written in paragraph prose and _entirely in your own words_.
4. Initial Proposed Approaches
    - Provide high-level descriptions for at least 2 potential approaches to innovate on the research topic see "Suggestions for Brainstorming Project Ideas" above for concrete advice be sure to consider compute requirements, and provide an estimate in your report.
5. Initial Proposed Data
    - Identify data sources that will be useful for training (if applicable) and evaluation.
6. Plans for Baseline Reproduction
    - As part of Assignment 4, you will implement at least two baseline approaches. Describe the baselines you intend to implement in this section.
    - You may choose describe more than 2 baselines, and then use your meeting with your assigned TA (see below) to narrow these down to the most sensible two.
    - You must cite a specific paper for each baseline. If a paper proposes multiple models or tasks, identify the specific variants you plan to reproduce.
7. Initial Proposed Evaluation
    - Which concepts or theoretical constructs are most relevant for evaluating your method, and why?
    - If the constructs can’t be directly measured, what metrics will serve as acceptable proxies?
    - If your system consists of multiple components, what constructs/metrics will you use to evaluate the sub-components?
    - What qualitative analyses do you plan to conduct?

### Peer Feedback (due November 10, 11:59 PM)

Shortly after the project proposal deadline, you will be assigned two proposals of your peers to provide feedback on. In your feedback, you should address at least 1 of the following.

- Do you have recommendations for relevent academic literature the team might have missed?
- Were any parts of the proposal confusing or underspecified?
- Do you have advice for how the methods or evaluation could be improved?
- Do you have suggestions for additional experiments the team could run?

Your feedback should be between 200 and 1,000 words. It should be written in your own words, without the use of AI.

### Meet with Assigned TA (due November 24, 11:59 PM)
Based on abstracts, we will assign your project a TA mentor.
Between November 10 and November 24, you should meet with your assigned TA at least once to present and discuss your plans for the project.
Your assigned TA will email you to schedule this meeting.

The purpose of the meeting is to ensure that your team is on track to a successful project, practice presenting a work-in-progress research project, check the proposed work is feasible within your resource constraints (compute, API credits, etc.), and just generally get some early feedback from your team's assigned TA.
You will have approximately 15 minutes with your TA. You should aim to present for about 10 minutes to ensure a few minutes are left for questions and discussion.

Your presentation should cover the following:

- introduce the research topic
- discuss key related work, including trends and shortcomings or opportunities to intervene
- your plan for baseline reproduction
- your initial ideas for new methods to address the research topic
  - What's one specific experiment you could run to test your idea?

Due to the time constraint, it is essential to be concise yet informative in your presentation and your responses. These are key skills in communicating effectively in virtually any research or industry context. We recommend that you prepare a slide deck for the presentation. You can present directly from one of your teammates' laptops.
